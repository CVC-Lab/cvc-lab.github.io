Paper 1 / Longitudinal prediction

Validity Gates Expose Limits of Longitudinal Multimodal Parkinson Disease Prediction

Similar motor scores can sit on very different evidence histories. This study makes those differences visible, then tests whether they support a trustworthy forecast. A small predictive gain is not the same as a clinically useful prediction.

Harsh Milind Tirhekar, Priyanshi Yadav, Chandrajit Bajaj

Motor scores of 10 and 11. Different evidence behind them.

P1-A has recent imaging and cognitive measurements; P1-B has no eligible DaT-SPECT value but does have injury and lysosomal assay evidence. A missing measurement is not a normal result. Neither record supports a treatment recommendation.

The two complete measured histories are also available in the patient comparison figure.

Selection, score meaning and uncertainty

The pair was selected without future outcomes or forecast errors. The earliest eligible records were matched within sex and analysis fold, with baseline calipers for motor score (3 points), age (5 years), disease duration (2 years) and prior motor slope (2 points/year). The closest clinical pair among 30 candidates was retained. Treatment was not matched; this is an illustration, not a causal comparison.

Evidence coordinates are oriented, training-fold-relative ranks on a scale from -1 to +1, not probabilities or clinical severity grades. Positive values indicate relatively greater evidence on that axis. Reliability summarizes available source weights, with a 24-month age half-life; it is not the probability of a correct forecast. An older source can retain its relative score while losing reliability.

The displayed prediction interval belongs only to the separately fitted nested uncertainty model, not to the main point forecasts. Valid interval coverage did not establish reliable error ranking. The cohort-level selective-uncertainty verdict is ABSTAIN; this is not an individualized diagnosis or a claim that either patient failed a test.

The scientific output is the decision boundary. Dated multimodal history contains a reproducible internal increment, but the effect is clinically tiny, temporally fragile, and not externally transportable in the available export. The protocol therefore stops the claim before deployment language begins.

Study design

Every forecast begins at a dated cutoff

Each patient's record is paused at a dated cutoff. Only measurements already available at that moment may describe the state. Their age and absence remain visible; the future motor examination, 9 to 15 months later, stays hidden until evaluation.

Why ordinary validation is insufficient

A repeated clinical history is not a flat table

The same person appears at multiple visits. A scan may be months older than the examination it accompanies. Missingness may encode a study protocol rather than biology. Medication state changes motor scores, and a new enrollment era can change both who is measured and how sources are collected.

These are different scientific risks. The benchmark therefore assigns each one a distinct population, uncertainty interval, and promotion rule.

Analysis Scale Question it answers
Rolling-origin benchmark 1,058 people; 5,404 cutoffs Do dated sources add internal information when every person stays in one fold?
Strict-calendar challenge 442 later-era participants Does the frozen relation survive a later enrollment and observation regime?
Site-disjoint challenge 951 people across 52 sites Is the increment dependent on acquisition center?
Gate stress test 8,000 controlled experiments Does the protocol suppress false promotion and detect leakage or shift?
PDBP clinical transport 77 calibration; 156 untouched test participants Does a frozen clinical model travel to an independent cohort?

A statistically stable improvement can still be practically negligible. The 0.060 point MAE reduction does not establish individual prognosis, patient-management value, or clinical-trial enrichment.

Uncertainty and stress testing

An interval may cover well and still fail to identify safer predictions

Coverage asks whether the future falls inside an interval. Selective prediction asks a harder question: when the model says it is more certain, is it actually less wrong?

External transport

A large archive is not the same as a compatible cohort

PDBP contains valuable Parkinson disease data, but a transport claim requires the same diagnosis, modalities, timing, endpoint, and eligible participants to intersect. File presence alone is not validation.

What this work contributes

  • A future-only rolling-origin patient representation
  • Separate people for model fitting, calibration, and testing
  • Independent gate verdicts instead of one favorable score
  • Executable stopping decisions under null effects, leakage, and shift

What this work does not claim

  • Individual clinical prognosis
  • Calibrated mechanism probability
  • Treatment selection or treatment benefit
  • Successful external multimodal validation
