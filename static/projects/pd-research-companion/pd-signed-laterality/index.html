Paper 2 / Signed biological evidence

Signed Dopaminergic Asymmetry Tracks Contralateral Motor Laterality in Parkinson's Disease

The amount of asymmetry does not tell us which side is affected. Retaining its direction distinguishes opposite biological states and exposes disagreement between imaging and examination. It helps describe the present more clearly than it predicts the future.

Harsh Milind Tirhekar, Priyanshi Yadav, Chandrajit Bajaj

Sign convention, case selection and model inputs

Signed imaging is (right SBR - left SBR) / (right SBR + left SBR). Signed motor laterality is (right paired motor sum - left paired motor sum) / (right paired motor sum + left paired motor sum). Thus positive imaging means relatively lower left putamen binding; positive motor laterality means greater right-body burden. Lower binding on one side is not a measurement of global disease severity.

The four frozen cases came from the 32-record strict multimodal pool within 1,204 longitudinal participants. Baseline state, source freshness, held-out model disagreement, interval width and a hashed tie-breaker were used, not the individual's future value or forecast error. A and B are not severity-matched. The fuller selection map follows below.

Clinical scores, demographics, examination state and LEDD enter the clinical model; imaging is added in the comparison models. MoCA, SAA and CSF values are contextual annotations, not inputs to these focused forecasts. CSF percentiles are relative to the 32-record illustration pool, not clinical reference ranges.

The 90% interval and its median belong to a separate conformalized quantile-regression model. They must not be attached to the four ridge-model point forecasts. Points on the history trace are actual examinations; connecting segments are not measured intervening states or a simulation of disease.

Directional coordinate

A side-aware representation retains anatomical meaning

Positive imaging values are fixed to relatively lower left putamen binding; positive clinical values are fixed to greater right-body burden. The orientation is defined before analysis and is carried unchanged across PPMI, S4, and future outcomes.

Outcome-blind selection audit

The illustrative histories were chosen before their futures were viewed

Thirty-two of 1,204 future-only participants met the strict same-month and multimodal completeness gate. Four baseline information states were defined in advance. One record was selected from each state using only landmark evidence, source freshness, model disagreement, interval width, and a hashed tie-breaker.

Biological replication

Replication supports a transferable anatomical coordinate. It does not establish a causal mechanism, clinical diagnosis, or treatment target by itself.

Making disagreement visible

Imaging and clinical direction do not always agree

A useful state must preserve disagreement rather than forcing every source into one label. The analysis therefore separates records whose imaging and clinical signs agree, disagree, or contain weak directional evidence.

Claim boundary

What sign adds, and what it does not

Which patients would an unsigned summary merge? A secondary audit identified 365 non-overlapping patient pairs with similar total motor scores, mean putamen binding and absolute asymmetry, but opposite imaging signs. In 281 pairs, the clinical signs were opposite too. Retaining direction keeps these biological states distinguishable.

These are not 365 misdiagnoses. The clinical reference already includes clinical laterality. In the full future-outcome comparison, binary side accuracy was 90.2% for the clinical model and 89.8% after adding signed imaging: no improvement. Nor did the earlier matched-profile analysis establish that secondary biomarkers explain future divergence. More informative representation, better prediction and biological explanation remain separate questions.
